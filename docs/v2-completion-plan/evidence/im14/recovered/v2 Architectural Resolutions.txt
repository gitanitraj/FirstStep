1. Purpose
This document formally records two approved architectural resolutions:
Community Cardinality: The relationship between Community and CivicContent, including its interpretation for multi-community capability.
Featured-Topic P5 Alignment: The complete deterministic selection hierarchy governing featured Topics and related featured-item collision behavior.
These resolutions clarify the authoritative implementation baseline and eliminate conflicting or incomplete interpretations in earlier architecture and execution-planning documents.
Both resolutions are approved. Neither is an open design question.
They do not amend the v2 Completion Contract, reopen the approved P5 logical model, introduce new domain entities, or authorize V3 capabilities.
2. Resolution A — Community Cardinality
Decision status: APPROVED AND RESOLVED
2.1 Authoritative cardinality
The approved relationship is:
Community 1 → many CivicContent records
Each CivicContent record belongs to exactly one Community.
Community represents the First Step catalog and delivery partition in which content is managed and presented.
Community does not represent the complete geographic reach, service area, jurisdiction, or resident eligibility of the underlying program, law, organization, or event.
Consequently, Community ownership must not be used to infer whether a resident is eligible for a particular program or service.
2.2 Multi-community capability
First Step v2 must be architecturally capable of maintaining Community A's catalog and Community B's catalog simultaneously.
Resident-facing operations must establish the relevant Community before querying or selecting content from its catalog.
This is a requirement for community-scoped catalog operations, not for multiple Community memberships on the same CivicContent record.
2.3 Required v2 implementation behavior
Preserve singular communityId / Community ownership.
Maintain Community as a first-class domain concept.
Validate Community references and catalog partition integrity.
Require Community-scoped resident retrieval.
Prevent implicit Wilmington-only assumptions in shared services and selection logic.
Keep Community ownership distinct from physical location, service area, and provider-published eligibility information.
2.4 Not required in v2
The following are explicitly outside the approved v2 requirement:
CivicContent–Community many-to-many associations.
Cross-community publication and syndication.
Community CRUD or onboarding infrastructure.
A Community administration system.
Production launch of a second community.
Global content identity shared across multiple Community catalogs.
2.5 Future escalation condition
The cardinality model may be reconsidered only when an approved requirement demonstrates that one canonical CivicContent identity must be published into multiple Community partitions.
Possible future approaches, such as a global content identity with Community-specific publication associations, remain future architectural considerations and are not authorized v2 work.
2.6 Execution matrix consequences
IM-12: Audit and verify singular Community ownership, community-scoped queries, valid references, and the absence of implicit Wilmington-only assumptions. Multiple independent Community partitions must be architecturally supportable.
IM-13: Include community-scoping and partition-isolation regression evidence without requiring a second-community production launch.
IM-14: Record Community cardinality as resolved. Do not retain GEO-01-C as an open cardinality decision.
The existing IM package dependencies and acceptance-evidence requirements remain unchanged.

3. Resolution B — Featured-Topic P5 Alignment
Decision status: APPROVED AND RESOLVED
3.1 Governing principle
Help first, context second.
Featured Topics must reflect the strength of relevant, resident-actionable information and opportunities, rather than simply the total number of classified content records.
Relevant law and policy information may help distinguish Topics when appropriate, but contextual information must not displace stronger resident-actionable inventory solely because it is more numerous or more recent.
3.2 Authoritative selection hierarchy
IM-06 must preserve the complete approved P5 ordering:
Resident-actionable inventory: Strength/count of current qualifying programs, services, resources, and access opportunities.
Featured-role completeness: Availability of qualifying TIMELY, DURABLE, and VISUAL content.
Direct resident-action opportunities: Strength of meaningful opportunities for residents to take a next step.
Relevant law or policy context: Explicitly classified and meaningfully related legal or policy information, used as a later discriminator.
Catalog/approval newness: Newer qualifying content only after stronger criteria are equal.
Stable Topic key: Immutable deterministic fallback independent of collection iteration order.
Admin diagnostic: Report cases where selection reaches a technical rather than meaningful fallback, without interrupting resident delivery.
This hierarchy is deterministic and based on approved, explicit metadata.
It does not create an AI ranking system, engagement score, personalized recommendation model, or permanent featured property.
3.3 IM-06 — Deterministic Featured-Topic Selector
Execution class: Implementation Required Dependencies: IM-01, IM-03, IM-05 Authority: Approved 2B-P5 §§18–19, core invariants, Decision 051 Pass 2A, J2, E2/E5
Required deliverables
Implement a reusable selector choosing up to four featured Topics for a Category Destination.
The selector must:
Establish Community and Category scope before evaluating candidates.
Use only controlled Topics belonging to that Category.
Evaluate current, resident-eligible inventory.
Exclude expired items while preserving otherwise eligible approaching-expiry items.
Apply the complete approved hierarchy in §3.2.
Avoid duplicate CivicContent counting caused by multiple projection memberships.
Return up to four Topics without manufacturing classifications or persisting permanent featured flags.
Support multiple Categories and Community partitions without Housing-specific hard-coding.
Acceptance tests
Identical catalog state and scope yield identical Topic selections and ordering.
Different input iteration orders cannot change results.
Expired content does not contribute to current inventory.
Approaching-expiry content remains eligible.
Context-heavy Topics cannot outrank stronger help-oriented Topics solely through law/news volume.
Role completeness resolves applicable actionable-inventory ties.
Law/policy context is considered only when explicitly classified and relevant to the Topic.
Newness follows stronger resident-relevant discriminators.
Stable Topic-key fallback produces the appropriate diagnostic.
Uncontrolled metadata cannot create new Topics.
Selection works with Housing and at least one additional Category fixture.
Selection remains Community-scoped.
Required evidence
Repository files inspected, selector implementation, named unit/integration tests, current Housing inventory comparison, collision fixtures, executed commands, test results, and final IM-06 disposition.
3.4 IM-07 — Featured-Item Selector Alignment
IM-07 must preserve approved P5-R6a through P5-R6d.
The three presentation roles remain:
TIMELY — meaningful near-term significance.
DURABLE — stable, currently useful information.
VISUAL — preference for an eligible relevant Flyer.
The implementation must maximize distinct role coverage before ordinary within-role tie-breaking.
A CivicContent record may qualify for multiple roles, but may not occupy two positions within the same featured Topic column.
If a Flyer qualifies for both TIMELY and VISUAL and another appropriate TIMELY candidate is available, the Flyer should ordinarily occupy VISUAL to preserve distinct-role coverage.
The selector must retain deterministic missing-role fallback and same-role collision resolution, including the approved distinction among direct-help, contextual, and stewardship temporal signals.
No new ContentType or persistent featured-content entity is authorized.
3.5 IM-11 — Real-Catalog Validation
IM-11 must verify both IM-06 and IM-07 against the then-current Housing catalog.
Required validation includes:
Actual selected featured Topics.
Featured role coverage.
Duplicate projection counting.
Same-role collisions.
Multi-role candidate handling.
Stable fallback behavior.
Relevant, explicitly classified law/policy context.
Regression tests for real catalog collisions.
Ordinary data collisions do not reopen P5. A genuine missing domain concept requires explicit architectural escalation supported by evidence.
